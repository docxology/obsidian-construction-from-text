*   **Vector ID:** M1

### **1.1 System Description**

*   **Vector ID:** M1.1
*   **Vector Type:** Description
    *   Content: The system is a collective of heterogeneous magnetic microrobots (circular microdisks of different sizes) at a fluid-air interface. These microrobots self-organize under an externally applied oscillating magnetic field. Each microrobot spins or oscillates, creating hydrodynamic and magnetic dipole-dipole interactions with neighbors. The system can achieve programmable self-organization, controlling density, morphology, and interaction with passive objects based on microrobot size and magnetic field parameters. The purpose is to explore self-organization in heterogeneous collectives for programmable self-assembly and active matter applications.

### **4.2 Local Interaction Rules:**

*   **Vector ID:** M4.2
*   **Vector Type:** Rules
    *   Content: The local interaction rules are defined by pairwise forces between microdisks:
        1.  **Attractive Magnetic Dipole-Dipole Force (F<sub>magdp</sub>):**  This force is attractive and acts along the center-to-center axis of two disks (i and j). It's given by equation [3]:
            F<sub>ij</sub><sup>magdp</sup>(d) = − (3μ<sub>0</sub> m<sub>i</sub> m<sub>j</sub>) / (4πd<sup>4</sup>) where m<sub>i</sub> = ρ<sub>m</sub>πR<sub>i</sub><sup>2</sup> and m<sub>j</sub> = ρ<sub>m</sub>πR<sub>j</sub><sup>2</sup>. R<sub>i</sub> and R<sub>j</sub> are radii, ρ<sub>m</sub> is magnetic moment per unit area, d is center-to-center distance, and μ<sub>0</sub> is vacuum permeability.
        2.  **Repulsive Hydrodynamic Force (F<sub>hydro</sub>):** This is repulsive and acts along the center-to-center axis. It's given by equation [2]:
            F<sub>ij</sub><sup>hydro</sup>(d) = (ρR<sub>i</sub><sup>4</sup>R<sub>j</sub><sup>3</sup>ω<sup>2</sup>) / d<sup>3</sup> where ρ is water density, R<sub>i</sub> and R<sub>j</sub> are radii, ω is angular velocity of disk j, and d is center-to-center distance.  Angular velocity ω is determined by the external magnetic field frequency Ω.
        3.  **Curvature Force:**  While not explicitly formulated as pairwise interaction, arena curvature creates a radial force pushing disks towards the center. (Mentioned in "System Overview" section as "disk–boundary repulsion... drives the disks to coalesce at the center").  This is more of a single-disk interaction with the environment's boundary condition.

    *   CT-GIN Mapping: Part of `AdjunctionEdge` description (local side). These define the "LocalInteraction" category of edges.  `LocalInteractionEdge: MagneticDipoleDipole`, attributes: `equation: [3]`, `forceType: Attractive`, `dependency: radius, distance, magnetic moment`; `LocalInteractionEdge: HydrodynamicRepulsion`, attributes: `equation: [2]`, `forceType: Repulsive`, `dependency: radius, distance, angular velocity, fluid density`; `EnvironmentalInteractionEdge: CurvatureForce`, attributes: `forceDirection: Radial inwards`, `environmentProperty: arena curvature`

### **4.3 Global Order:**

*   **Vector ID:** M4.3
*   **Vector Type:** Order
    *   Content: The global order that emerges is spatial separation of microrobots by size.  Specifically, for lower magnetic field frequencies (Ω < Ω<sub>s</sub>), larger disks tend to aggregate towards the center of the collective, while smaller disks are pushed to the periphery, forming concentric rings of size-segregated disks. The overall shape of the collective can also be controlled, from circular to elliptical, based on magnetic field parameters and disk heterogeneity.
