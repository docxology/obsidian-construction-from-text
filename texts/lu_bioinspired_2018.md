### **1.1 System Description**

*   **Vector ID:** M1.1
*   **Vector Type:** Description
    *   Content: The system is a bioinspired untethered soft millirobot. It is designed with multiple tapered soft feet architecture, mimicking legs and feet structures found in living organisms. The robot is fabricated from polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) and iron microparticles using a modified magnetic particle-assisted molding approach. It is actuated remotely by an external magnetic field. The robot exhibits two main locomotion modes: discontinuous flap-wave (DFW) and continuous inverted-pendulum (CIP), enabling it to move in both dry and wet conditions, carry loads exceeding 100 times its own weight, achieve ultrafast locomotion speed (>40 limb length/s), and overcome obstacles. The purpose is to create a versatile millirobot for industrial and biomedical applications operating in harsh environments.

Provide specific, actionable feedback on the *CT-GIN template itself*, based on this analysis:
    *   **Missing Probes:** The template is quite comprehensive, but probes for "scalability of fabrication" and "long-term operational stability/degradation" could be added to Module M1 (System Overview & Implementation) and Module M8 (Emergent Behaviors), respectively, as these are crucial aspects for real-world material intelligence systems.  Also, adding probes specifically for "control mechanism" in M1 could be useful (e.g., manual, programmed, feedback loop).
    *   **Unclear Definitions:** The definition of "Active Inference" in M6.2 and "Adaptive Plasticity" in M7.1 are complex and might benefit from slightly simplified, more operational definitions or examples tailored to material systems directly within the template itself, rather than relying solely on background documents. The distinction between 'designed' and 'emergent' order in M4.1 could be further clarified with an example scenario contrasting the two in a material system context.
    *   **Unclear Node/Edge Representations:** The guidance for mapping to GIN nodes/edges is sufficient, but providing a few example mappings within the template itself (perhaps linking back to the CT-GIN Readiness Summary Table aspects as potential node types) could further clarify the process for new users.
    *   **Scoring Difficulties:** Assigning scores for cognitive functions and particularly the cognitive proximity score (M9.2, M9.3) was challenging due to the abstract nature of "cognition" applied to a simple robotic system. The rubrics are helpful, but more fine-grained criteria within each level of the Cognizance Scale, especially for the lower levels (0-3), could improve inter-rater reliability and reduce subjectivity.
    *   **Data Extraction/Output Mapping:** Data extraction was straightforward for quantifiable parameters. However, mapping qualitative assessments (like robustness, criticality) directly to numerical scores requires careful justification and could be more subjective. Clearer guidance on converting qualitative judgments into numerical scores, potentially using Likert-style scales or providing more detailed descriptive anchors for each score point, could be beneficial.
    *   **Overall Usability:** The template is structurally comprehensive and well-organized. However, for complex papers, the sheer number of probes can be somewhat daunting. Perhaps grouping closely related probes visually within sections (e.g., using indentation or boxes) could improve visual parsing and navigation within the template during analysis.

Refine definitions of Active Inference, Adaptive Plasticity and 'designed' vs 'emergent' order. Add example GIN node/edge mappings in the template. Provide more granular criteria within the Cognizance Scale rubrics, especially for levels 0-3. Add guidance on converting qualitative assessments to scores. Improve visual organization by grouping related probes.
