### **2.2 Energy Transduction**

*   **Vector ID:** M2.2
*   **Vector Type:** Transduction
    *   Content:  Electrical energy is supplied to the Helmholtz coils to generate the precessing magnetic field. This magnetic field interacts with the ferromagnetic carbonyl iron microparticles within the droplets, inducing magnetic dipole moments in the particles. The magnetic dipole-dipole interaction between particles leads to the formation of particle chains. The precessing field causes these chains, and consequently the droplets, to rotate.  The rotation of droplets leads to hydrodynamic interactions and self-assembly of droplets into patterns. Applying a magnetic field gradient transduces magnetic energy into translational kinetic energy, allowing for steering.

### **4.2 Local Interaction Rules:**

*   **Vector ID:** M4.2
*   **Vector Type:** Rules
    *   Content: The local interaction rules are described as:
        1.  **Magnetic Dipole-Dipole Interaction:**  Induced by the precessing magnetic field, this interaction is repulsive and described by equation (2):  `F (θ, r) = −(3μ om d2)/(4πr4 ) * (3cos2θ − 1)/2`. This force depends on the precession angle `θ` and inter-droplet distance `r`. It can be repulsive or attractive depending on the angle `θ`.
        2.  **Hydrodynamic Interaction:**  Arises from droplet rotation in the fluid environment and calculated using equation (3): `F = −cρω2 d i j a7(r − r) / (|r − r|4)`. This force is repulsive and depends on droplet angular velocity `ω_d`, fluid density `ρ_f`, droplet radius `a`, and inter-droplet distance vector `r_i - r_j`.
        3.  **Capillary Attraction:** Operates between droplets at the air-liquid interface, described qualitatively and by equation (1): `F(r) ∝ exp(−r/L_c)/(r^(1/2)L_c^(1/2))`. This force is attractive and depends on inter-droplet distance `r` and capillary length `L_c`.
        The interplay and balance of these three forces determine the self-assembled pattern.
