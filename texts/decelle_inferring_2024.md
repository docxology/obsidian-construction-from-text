## M1: System Overview & Implementation
*   **Vector ID:** M1

### **1.1 System Description**

*   **Vector ID:** M1.1
*   **Vector Type:** Description
    *   Content: The system is a computational framework utilizing Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) to infer effective couplings in generalized Ising Models (GIMs). The RBM is employed as a generative model to learn from data samples generated by predefined Ising models (1D and 2D Ising models, Edwards-Anderson model, random graph Ising model). The core components are the RBM architecture (visible and hidden units, weights, biases) and the mathematical mapping derived to translate RBM parameters into GIM Hamiltonian parameters, encompassing interactions of all orders. The purpose is to establish a physically interpretable method for inferring interaction networks from binary variable datasets, extending beyond pairwise interactions and overcoming limitations of traditional Boltzmann Machines.

### **7.2 Adaptation Mechanism:**

*   **Vector ID:** M7.2
*   **Vector Type:** Description
    *   Content: Gradient Ascent on Log-Likelihood Function. The RBM adapts through iterative updates of its weights (W) and biases (b, c) using gradient ascent to maximize the log-likelihood of the training dataset (Eq 25, 26, Appendix A.2). The gradient is estimated using Persistent Contrastive Divergence (PCD-K) or Rdm-K methods involving MCMC sampling (Appendix A.2).  The direction and magnitude of parameter changes are determined by the gradient of the log-likelihood, guiding the model towards parameter configurations that better represent the data distribution. This is a form of statistical learning, specifically maximum likelihood estimation, driven by feedback from the training data. The learning rate (γ) controls the step size of parameter adjustments.

*   **Level 1: Simple Responsivity:** Basic stimulus-response behavior.  Reactions are fixed and predetermined.
*   **Level 2: Sub-Organismal Responsivity:**  Behavior exhibits basic forms of adaptation or plasticity, but lacks complex representation or goal-directedness. **[APPLICABLE LEVEL]**
*   **Level 3: Reactive/Adaptive Autonomy:** System adapts its behavior based on experience and feedback, but within a limited behavioral repertoire.
*   **Level 4: Goal-Directed/Model-Based Cognition:** System exhibits goal-directed behavior based on internal models of the world, allowing for planning and flexible action selection.
*   **Level 5: Contextual/Relational Cognition:** System understands and responds to relationships between objects, events, and concepts.
*   **Level 6: Abstract/Symbolic Cognition:** System can manipulate abstract concepts and symbols, enabling logical reasoning and problem-solving.
*   **Level 7: Social Cognition:** System exhibits social intelligence, understanding and interacting with other agents.
*   **Level 8: Metacognition/Self-Awareness:** System possesses awareness of its own internal states and cognitive processes.
*   **Level 9: Phenomenal Consciousness:**  System exhibits subjective experience (qualia). (Currently theoretical for material systems)
*   **Level 10: Sapience/Self-Reflective Consciousness:** System possesses self-awareness, understanding of its own existence, and ability for complex abstract thought. (Currently theoretical for material systems)
