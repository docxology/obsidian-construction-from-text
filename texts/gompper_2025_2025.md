### **4.2 Local Interaction Rules:**

*   **Vector ID:** M4.2
*   **Vector Type:** Rules
    *   Content: The roadmap mentions various local interaction rules, depending on the specific active matter system:
        * **Alignment and attraction/repulsion (Sections 12, 14, 17):** In swarming and flocking models, particles adjust their motion based on the position and orientation of neighbors, including alignment, attraction, and repulsion forces. Specific models include Vicsek, Boid, and Zonal models. For example, in iABP model the steering torques (Equations provided in section 14) are based on relative positions and orientations of neighbors.
         * **Hydrodynamic interactions (Sections 1, 7, 8):** Microswimmers interact through fluid flows they generate, leading to bound states, collective flows, and long-range interactions. For example, chiral microswimmers interact hydrodynamically.
        * **Chemical signaling (Sections 1, 5, 6, 11, 15, 19):** Chemically propelled particles and biological systems interact via chemical gradients, leading to chemotaxis, quorum sensing, and complex dynamics and feedback loops. Bacteria communicate via quorum sensing.
        * **Steric interactions (Sections 2, 5, 14):** Short-range repulsions and excluded volume effects between particles influence collective organization, especially in dense systems and confined environments.
        * **Non-reciprocal interactions (Sections 2, 7, 13):** Specific interactions are described as non-reciprocal, going beyond Newton's third law and leading to novel states, e.g., non-reciprocal Cahn-Hilliard model.
        * **Vision-induced and alignment-induced steering (Sections 14, 16):** In cognitive swarming models, agents are described to use vision or directional sensing to steer towards or align with neighbors, which are formalized as vision and alignment induced torques.
    *   CT-GIN Mapping: Part of the `AdjunctionEdge` description (local side). These define the "LocalInteraction" category of edges.
    * **Implicit/Explicit**: Mixed
        *  Justification: While the paper explicitly mentions the *types* of local interactions, the *precise rules* (equations, algorithms) are often only implicitly referenced through mentions of specific models (Vicsek, Boid, iABP) and phenomena (chemotaxis, hydrodynamic interactions).  The iABP model provides explicit equations for steering torques, making it mixed.

### **4.3 Global Order:**

*   **Vector ID:** M4.3
*   **Vector Type:** Order
    *   Content: The roadmap describes diverse forms of global order emerging from local interactions:
        * **Swarming:** Cohesive group motion in bacteria, birds, fish, and microrobots (Sections 14, 16, 17, 19), including collective problem solving by swarms.
        * **Flocking:** Polar ordered motion with alignment, observed in chiral active matter (Sections 8, 13).
        * **Milling:** Circular motion patterns in swarms (Section 14, 17), observed in simulations and animal groups.
        * **Rotating macrodroplets:** Dense, rotating clusters in chiral active matter with polar alignment (Section 8).
        * **Microflock patterns:** Length-scale selected patterns in chiral active matter (Section 8).
        * **Traveling bands & waves:** Density and orientation waves in active matter (Sections 8, 13).
        * **Vortices & Foams:** Polar vortices in active matter with polar alignment and active foams in active matter with nematic alignment (Section 8).
        * **Active crystals:** Ordered structures formed by chiral microswimmers (Section 7, 9), such as starfish embryos.
        * **Active gels & solids:** Elastic materials formed by active components, exhibiting wave propagation, actuation, etc. (Sections 9, 13).
        * **Phase separation:** Motility-induced phase separation (MIPS) in active matter (Sections 1, 3, 4, 8, 14).
